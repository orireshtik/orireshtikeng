In December 2020 the Israeli business daily Globes named Ori Reshtik, then CEO of Shaham, the Israeli Actors Union, to its "40 Under 40" list. The article about him ran on the Globes website on 28 December 2020, and in the print edition of G magazine on 31 December 2020.

What is the Globes 40 Under 40 list?

Every year Globes publishes its young leadership project, "40 Under 40", which names Israel's most promising people aged 40 and under. 2020 was its 15th year. Past honorees named by Globes include Rakefet Roussak-Aminoach, Lilach Asher-Topilsky, Gigi Levy, Kira Radinsky and Ran Balicer.

The 2020 list was different from earlier years. Globes dedicated it to young people who showed exceptional leadership during COVID, in health, in the economy and in society. It also opened nominations to the public, and more than 800 people wrote in. A 15-member advisory committee, all of them past honorees, helped choose the final list.

The article in brief

Article by Liat Ron, in Hebrew. This is a summary written for this site, not a translation.

Globes describes him as having "the managerial worldview of an experienced businessman", and tells the story of how he ran Shaham through the year the culture sector shut down.

  • A small amendment that paid off in the crisis. He found a 1972 National Insurance order that treats some self-employed workers as employees. In 2018, working with Lahav, the chamber of self-employed organizations, he secured an amendment covering actors with a contract of three months or more, or five or more performances. When COVID hit, those actors were almost the only self-employed workers in Israel to receive unemployment benefits from National Insurance.
  • Moving before the shutdown. Two days before the limit on gatherings of 100 people, he told all actors to register with the employment service, and Shaham opened a crisis room. It walked them through unemployment claims and then worked with National Insurance on the payments.
  • Getting people back to work. He negotiated a quick return to work for film and television under the restrictions. Shaham agreed in return that shoots cancelled because of COVID would carry no compensation for actors.
  • A fund with no overhead. Shaham set up its own donation system at a cost of NIS 2,000, so that donations went straight to artists. By the time of the article it had raised NIS 1.8 million, handed out by set criteria to those who applied.
  • Building the organization. After he became CEO at the end of 2014, Shaham widened its representation from television and film to repertory theater, children's theater, fringe and dubbing. Membership grew from 1,800 to 2,500.

Since then

  • 2021 to 2026: CEO of Eshkolot, the performers' rights company of Israeli artists.
  • Today: Chief Business & Operations Officer at Singit.
